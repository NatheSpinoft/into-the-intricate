Boredom & the Brain

The Hidden Value of Being Bored

September 1, 2025

Most people see boredom as something negative — a waste of time, a signal that nothing exciting is happening. But research suggests the opposite: boredom can actually be good for the brain.

When the mind isn’t occupied with constant stimulation, it has the chance to wander. This downtime activates what’s called the default mode network, the part of the brain tied to imagination, memory, and self-reflection. In other words, boredom creates the space where new ideas can surface.

Psychologists have also noted that boredom often nudges us toward problem-solving. It’s the brain’s way of saying: “find something meaningful to do.” That little push can fuel creativity, spark reflection, or even motivate us to take on challenges we’ve been avoiding.

Next time you feel bored, instead of rushing to your phone or TV, sit with it. Let your brain stretch out in the quiet. You might be surprised at the clarity, creativity, and motivation that follows.
